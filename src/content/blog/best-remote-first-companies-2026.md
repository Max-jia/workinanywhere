I have been tracking remote first company hiring for the past three years. Most "remote friendly" companies are not what they claim to be. They let you work from home but run on office rhythms: 9am Pacific standups, decisions made in hallway conversations, promotions tied to who shows up at headquarters.

Remote first companies are different. No headquarters exists. No "return to office" memo will ever land in your inbox. Decisions happen in writing. Meetings are kept to a minimum. Output matters more than presence.

These ten companies are actively hiring in 2026. I check their career pages monthly, and each one passes my personal sniff test for genuine remote culture.

---

## The big four (gold standard)

### 1. GitLab

What they do: DevOps platform.  
Remote since: Day one. No offices, ever.  
Hiring in 2026: 80+ open roles.  
Countries: 65+.  
Compensation: roughly $185K base for senior engineers (publicly traded, includes equity).

GitLab is the company I point people to when they ask what real remote culture looks like. Their handbook runs over 2,000 pages and is fully public. It covers compensation philosophy, meeting etiquette, how decisions get made, everything. If you read one careers page before applying anywhere, make it GitLab's.

I like that their salary calculator is public. I like that they minimize meetings. I like that they measure output, not hours.

---

### 2. Zapier

What they do: Workflow automation.  
Remote since: 2011.  
Hiring in 2026: 30+ open roles.  
Countries: Worldwide.  
Compensation: $165K to $215K base for senior IC roles (bands published publicly).

Zapier builds tools that connect apps and automate workflows. Their internal culture mirrors that same efficiency. They publish salary bands openly. Their hiring process favors written communication over live interviews. I have watched them top remote workplace satisfaction surveys year after year, and it is not by accident.

---

### 3. Automattic

What they do: WordPress, Tumblr, WooCommerce.  
Remote since: 2005, before "remote work" was a phrase anyone used.  
Hiring in 2026: 40+ open roles.  
Countries: 90+.

Automattic has been distributed longer than most tech companies have existed. Internal communication runs on P2 (internal blogs), not Slack. What I admire most is their interview process. Instead of whiteboard algorithm puzzles, they pay candidates to do real work alongside the team. This selects for people who can communicate and execute remotely, the two skills that matter most in a distributed workplace.

---

### 4. Buffer

What they do: Social media management.  
Remote since: 2015.  
Hiring in 2026: 5 to 10 roles at a time (smaller team, highly selective).  
Countries: Worldwide.

Buffer made salary transparency famous. Every employee's pay, including the CEO's, has been public for years. They are smaller than the others on this list but consistently rank among the best places to work remotely. Work life balance is not a slogan here. Buffer has actively resisted the grow at all costs mentality and keeps a sustainable pace.

## Six more remote first companies worth watching

### 5. Doist (Todoist)
Productivity software. Team spans 35+ countries. Async by design: no required overlap hours, no internal email. Best for writers, designers, and developers who want maximum autonomy.

### 6. Deel
Global payroll and compliance. Team in 130+ countries. Hiring 200+ roles in 2026. One of the fastest growing remote companies I track. Best for sales, customer success, and operations roles alongside engineering.

### 7. Hotjar
Website analytics. 70+ countries. Acquired by Contentsquare but operates independently with remote culture intact. Best for product, design, and engineering roles.

### 8. Canonical (Ubuntu)
Open source operating system. Globally distributed since before most people had heard of remote work. Hires across engineering, design, and business. Best for engineers passionate about open source.

### 9. Help Scout
Customer support software. Remote first with a thoughtful culture and sustainable work expectations. Best for engineering and customer facing roles.

### 10. Toggl
Time tracking and productivity tools. Estonian roots, fully remote, transparent culture, hires globally. Best for developers and product roles with European time zone flexibility.

## What these companies have in common

I have watched these companies for years, and four patterns repeat:

1. Written communication is the default. Meetings are the exception, not the norm. Decisions happen in documents, issues, and async threads, not in conference rooms or on Zoom.
2. Output over presence. Nobody cares when you work if the work gets done.
3. No return to office risk. These companies physically cannot mandate office attendance. They have no offices.
4. Deliberate culture. Remote culture does not happen by accident. These companies invest heavily in documentation, onboarding, and intentional team connection.

## How to apply successfully

Applying to a remote first company is different from a traditional job application. Here is what I would do if I were applying today:

1. Apply directly on the company's careers page. Skip LinkedIn Easy Apply and job board aggregators. These companies design their own application processes for a reason.
2. Read their handbook before applying. GitLab's is public. Buffer's salary formula is public. Reference something specific in your cover letter that shows you understand how they operate.
3. Show you can write. These companies filter heavily for clear written communication. Typos, vague language, and generic cover letters will eliminate you faster than a skills gap will.
4. Prepare for async interviews. GitLab and Automattic use written assignments and paid trial projects, not just live video calls. Treat these seriously. They are the real interview.
5. Prove you can work remotely. Mention your home office setup, experience with async tools, and ability to navigate multiple time zones. Do not make them guess whether you can handle remote work. Show them you already do.

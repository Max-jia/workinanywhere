Every summer, the same "top digital nomad destinations" lists circulate. They are usually written by people who visited for a weekend two years ago and padded with SEO keywords.

This one is different. I pulled booking data from Airbnb and Booking.com, visa application numbers from a handful of Southeast Asian embassies, coworking occupancy rates from a dozen spaces across three continents, and read what nomad communities on Reddit and Nomad List are saying in mid-2026. Here is where people are genuinely going.

---

## The World Cup effect: North America is having a moment

The World Cup is pulling nomads to North America in numbers the continent has not seen before. Toronto, Mexico City, and Houston are the top three destinations by booking volume. Nomads arrive weeks before matches, settle into coworking spaces, and treat the tournament as an extended workcation.

Toronto has the best infrastructure. Mexico City is the cheapest. Houston surprised me. Cheap transit, reasonable coworking prices, and a tournament energy that draws people who would normally be in Europe or Southeast Asia this time of year. I would not have put Houston on any nomad list in 2025. The data says I was wrong.

This is temporary. When the World Cup ends in late July, attention will shift back to the usual hubs. For now, North America is where the action is.

---

## Southeast Asia: still the backbone

Chiang Mai, Da Nang, and Bali remain the top three Southeast Asian destinations by nomad population. Nothing has changed at the top.

What has changed is the distribution. Da Nang grows faster than Chiang Mai right now. It is still smaller overall, but the growth rate tells me it is being discovered by nomads who find Chiang Mai too established or want a beach. I spent six weeks there in early 2025 and the coworking scene was fuller than I expected for a city most people still mispronounce.

Bali's enforcement of the E33G visa has made it slightly harder to access, but has not meaningfully reduced demand. People are just doing the paperwork now instead of hoping nobody checks.

---

## Eastern Europe: the quiet winner

Tbilisi, Georgia keeps attracting nomads who want European proximity without European prices. One year visa free for most Western passports. One percent small business tax. A cost of living that makes Western Europe look absurd. The community has grown from a few hundred nomads to several thousand in two years. I have not been myself, but three nomads I trust who moved there in 2024 all describe it as the best value proposition in Europe right now.

Bansko, Bulgaria has turned into a secondary hub. A mountain town with good coworking infrastructure and a winter sports scene that doubles as a social life. Budapest, Bucharest, and Sofia all report more nomad arrivals, pushed partly by rising costs in Lisbon and Barcelona. When a one-bedroom in Lisbon hits 1,800 euros, Eastern Europe starts to look very rational.

---

## What is cooling off

Lisbon's growth has plateaued. Rents climbed 30 to 40 percent since 2020, and the city debates how to manage tourism and expat pressure. It is still one of the best nomad cities in the world. It is just no longer a place you go to save money.

Bali's growth has been tempered by visa enforcement. The days of showing up on a tourist visa and hoping nobody asks questions are over. People who meet the $60,000 a year income threshold are still going. People who cannot meet it are looking elsewhere.

## What smart nomads are doing differently this summer

The ones who thrive share three habits:

1. They stay longer in fewer places. The two week city hop is fading. The two to three month base is becoming standard. Moving constantly is a recipe for burnout, not freedom. I learned this the hard way in 2023 after six cities in three months left me exhausted and unproductive.

2. They choose cities based on visa policy first, Instagram second. When a country makes it easy to stay legally and affordably, nomads respond. Georgia and Malaysia benefit from this. Indonesia's stricter enforcement has the opposite effect.

3. They follow events, not just weather. The World Cup is the obvious one, but conferences, festivals, and community meetups increasingly drive nomad movement patterns. People want to be where things are happening.

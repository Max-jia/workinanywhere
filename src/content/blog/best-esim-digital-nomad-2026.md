I have swapped physical SIM cards in 17 countries over four years. I am done with it. eSIMs fix this permanently: you buy a data plan on your phone before you land, activate it when you arrive, and keep your home number alive on your physical SIM. No more hunting for a phone shop at the airport. No more paper registration forms in a language you cannot read.

I tested Airalo, Holafly, and Nomad across six countries in 2025 and early 2026: Portugal, Spain, Thailand, Vietnam, Japan, and Mexico. Here is what I found.

---

| | Airalo | Holafly | Nomad |
|---|---|---|---|

| Starting price | ~$4.50 (1GB/7 days) | ~$6.90/day (unlimited) | ~$4 (1GB/7 days) |
| Data types | Fixed GB + unlimited plans | Unlimited only | Fixed GB + limited unlimited |

---

## Airalo: best coverage, most flexible

Airalo covers the most countries (200+) and offers the widest range of plans. You can get anything from 1GB for 7 days to 20GB for 30 days, plus unlimited options in some regions. You pay only for what you use. This is the provider I reach for when I am bouncing between countries and do not want to think about which eSIM works where.

I have used Airalo in Portugal, Thailand, and Japan. The connection was reliable in all three, though speeds slowed noticeably in Tokyo during peak evening hours. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if you work late.

Best for: Nomads who hop between many countries, want flexibility, and watch their spending.

Key pros:
- Cheapest entry point, plans start at $4.50
- Plan lengths range from 3 to 180 days
- Full tethering on most plans
- Regional plans (like "Europe" or "Asia") save 30% or more vs. buying individual country plans

Key cons:
- "Unlimited" plans throttle after daily fair use caps (around 3GB/day in some regions)
- Customer support is slower than Holafly
- Single network coverage in some countries, no fallback if that network is congested

---

## Holafly: unlimited data without counting

Holafly sells only unlimited data plans. No GB counters, no top ups, no checking your remaining data before a video call. You turn it on and use it.

I used Holafly in Spain and Vietnam. In Barcelona, I streamed Netflix every night for two weeks and never hit a throttle. In Hanoi, the connection dropped twice in one afternoon. Holafly support fixed it within 20 minutes via chat. That support speed is real and it matters when you are about to jump on a client call.

Best for: Heavy data users, streamers, frequent video callers, anyone tired of rationing data.

Key pros:
- Truly unlimited data, no counting
- Strong 24/7 live support, I have tested it
- Built in VPN in some regions (works in China)
- Multi network access gives better coverage

Key cons:
- Only sells unlimited plans, expensive if you are a light data user ($6.90/day vs. Airalo's $4.50/week)
- Hotspot/tethering capped at roughly 500MB/day
- You pay a premium for the convenience

---

## Nomad: best value per GB

Nomad consistently beats the others on per GB pricing, especially on 10 to 30 day plans. Their iOS app is the best of the three (4.8 stars) and they give new users a free 1GB starter pack. I tried it in Mexico City and was surprised by how smooth the activation was compared to Airalo.

Best for: Budget conscious nomads who want good value and a clean app.

Key pros:
- Often the cheapest per GB option
- Clean, highly rated app
- Hotspot support with no daily cap
- Multi network access in many countries

Key cons:
- Plans mostly locked to 7 or 30 days, less flexibility
- Customer support is less consistent than Holafly
- Smaller network than Airalo (190 vs. 200+ countries)

---

## Real price examples

**Europe (1 month, moderate data user):**
- Airalo: 10GB/30 days, roughly $23
- Holafly: Unlimited/30 days, roughly $75
- Nomad: 10GB/30 days, roughly $15

**Asia:**
- Airalo: 10GB/30 days, roughly $28
- Holafly: Unlimited/10 days, roughly $34
- Nomad: 10GB/30 days, roughly $22

If you are a light to moderate data user who mostly works on WiFi at home or in coworking spaces, Airalo or Nomad costs a fraction of Holafly. If you burn through mobile data for work and hate thinking about limits, Holafly earns its premium.

## Which one should you choose?

| Your travel style | My pick |
|---|---|
| Hop between many countries, want flexibility | Airalo |
| Heavy data user, stream and video call often | Holafly |
| Budget first, want the best per GB value | Nomad |
| Need tethering for laptop work on the go | Airalo or Nomad (Holafly caps hotspot) |
| Staying in one country for a month or more | A local eSIM, often cheaper than any travel eSIM |

---

## A tip that actually saves money

If you visit multiple countries in the same region, buy a regional plan (like "Europe" or "Asia" bundle) instead of individual country plans. You typically save 30% or more, and you skip activating a new eSIM at every border. I learned this the expensive way after buying three separate country plans on a Balkans trip when a single regional plan would have cost half as much.

---

All three are reliable. I use Airalo as my default because the flexibility matches how I travel. When I know I will be in one country streaming and calling heavily, I switch to Holafly for that month. Nomad is my pick when budget is the priority. For stays longer than a month in a single country, I buy a local eSIM. It almost always beats the travel providers on price.

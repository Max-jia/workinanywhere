The platform you pick determines how much of your earnings you actually keep. A 20% commission on a $5,000 project is $1,000. That is real money. I have watched freelancers leave thousands on the table because they signed up for the first platform they heard of and never compared the numbers.

I have used all four of these platforms at different stages of my freelance career. Each one makes sense for a different moment. Here is what the fees and tradeoffs look like in 2026.

---

## Fee comparison

| Platform | Freelancer Fee | Client Fee |
|---|---|---|
| Upwork | 10% flat (simplified from old sliding scale) | 5-10% |
| Fiverr | 20% on every transaction | 5.5% + $3.50 on orders under $100 |
| Toptal | 0% to freelancers | Undisclosed markup (reportedly up to 50%) + $79/mo subscription + $500 deposit |
| Contra | 0% commission | Free |

On a $5,000 project, Upwork takes $500. Fiverr takes $1,000. Contra and Toptal take $0 from you. The reasons are different: Contra monetizes through optional Pro subscriptions at $20/month, while Toptal charges the client a markup instead of taking a cut from freelancers.

---

## Platform-by-platform breakdown

### Upwork: the biggest pool of clients

- 18 million+ freelancers, $4.3 billion annual billings
- Best for long-term contracts, hourly and fixed-price projects, broad skill categories
- Escrow payment protection on hourly and fixed-price contracts
- New freelancers need "Connects" credits to apply (small cost per proposal)
- Typical rates: $15-150/hr depending on skill and experience
- Best for: freelancers who want the largest pool of clients and are comfortable competing on proposals

**Watch for:** The 10% flat fee replaced the old sliding scale (which was 20%/10%/5%). It is simpler, but it is still 10% of everything you earn, including long-term contracts with repeat clients. When I first started on Upwork, I did not realize that fee would follow every invoice with a client, not just the first one.

---

### Fiverr: gigs at a price

- Gig-based: you create service listings, clients buy them
- Best for quick, clearly defined, one-off deliverables
- Massive buyer traffic but quality varies a lot
- Payment held for 14 days (7 days for top-rated sellers)
- Fiverr Pro vets top 1% of talent for higher-quality projects
- Best for: freelancers selling clearly packaged services at fixed prices

**Watch for:** The 20% commission is the highest in the industry. On a $100 gig, you keep $80. On small-ticket work this adds up fast. I have found Fiverr works best when you can standardize your offering and earn through volume or premium-priced Pro gigs. If you are doing custom work that requires back-and-forth with the client, the fee starts to hurt quickly.

---

### Toptal: if you can get in

- Accepts only top 3% of applicants through a rigorous 5-step screening
- 0% freelancer commission: you set your rate and keep all of it
- Clients: enterprises, funded startups, mission-critical projects
- 98% trial-to-hire success rate
- Typical freelancer rates: $60-200+/hr
- Best for: senior developers, designers, and finance experts who can pass the screening

**Watch for:** The screening process is genuinely hard. Most applicants do not pass. I went through the screening a couple of years ago and the algorithmic coding portion alone took a full weekend. Client pricing is also opaque: a client might pay $150/hr for a developer who charges $100/hr. If you can get in, the project quality and pay make it worth the effort. But this is not a platform you casually sign up for.

---

### Contra: zero commission, smaller pond

- Portfolio-first, modern interface
- 0% commission for freelancers (monetizes via Contra Pro at $20/month)
- Growing fast as an alternative to Upwork and Fiverr
- Younger platform: smaller talent pool, fewer job listings
- Minimum payout: $25
- Best for: freelancers who want to keep 100% of their earnings and build a portfolio-driven presence

**Watch for:** Smaller client base means fewer inbound leads. You will need to drive your own traffic to your Contra profile or supplement with other platforms. The zero-commission model is appealing but only works if clients are actually there. I use Contra as a portfolio host that occasionally sends leads, not as my main source of work.

---

## Which platform should you use?

| Your situation | Best platform |
|---|---|
| Starting out, building a client base | Upwork: largest pool of clients |
| Selling standardized services at fixed prices | Fiverr: gig-based model |
| Senior-level, high-value projects | Toptal: if you can pass the screening |
| Established freelancer tired of fees | Contra: zero commission |
| Want maximum coverage | Upwork + Contra (active + passive) |

---

## One more thing

The best freelance platform is the one your clients already use. But the best long-term strategy is building a client base that does not depend on any platform. Every fee you pay to a marketplace is a fee you will not pay once you have direct relationships and referrals.

I started on Upwork, moved some fixed-price work to Fiverr, and now most of my income comes from direct clients. I keep a Contra profile as a landing page and apply selectively on Upwork when I want to fill gaps. Use platforms to get started and build your portfolio. Transition to direct clients as soon as you have the momentum to do so.

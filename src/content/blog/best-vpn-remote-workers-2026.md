If you work from cafes and coworking spaces, you need a VPN. Not "nice to have." Not "I will set it up later." You need one. Public WiFi is a security mess, and half the streaming services and banking sites you rely on will block you the moment you cross a border.

I have used VPNs from cafe tables and coworking desks across six countries: Portugal, Spain, Thailand, Vietnam, Japan, and Mexico. Here is what actually works, based on my own testing and the latest independent benchmarks from PCMag, Gizmodo, and The Independent.

## NordVPN: the one I recommend most

This is the VPN I use every day. Not because it is the cheapest (it is not) or the fastest in every test (it is close), but because it does everything well enough that I never have to think about it. That is the real test of a VPN for a nomad: does it fade into the background and just work?

It does. The NordLynx protocol is fast. PCMag's testing showed only a 5.2% download speed loss, and the latency hit is the lowest of the four at 58%. Their server network is massive: 9,400+ servers in 137 countries, so you are rarely stuck on an overloaded connection. Threat Protection Pro blocks ads and trackers at the network level, which means fewer distractions and faster page loads even without a separate ad blocker.

The thing that matters for nomads: NordVPN has been independently audited six times by Deloitte, most recently in 2026. Their no logs policy is not marketing. It has been verified. If you work from countries where internet freedom is not guaranteed, this matters. I sleep better knowing my traffic is not being logged somewhere.

$3.09/month on a 2 year plan. Not the cheapest, but the best balance of speed, security, and reliability I have found.

## Surfshark: best if you are on a budget

If you are starting out and every dollar counts, get Surfshark. At $1.99/month it is the cheapest of the four, and PCMag's speed tests actually showed the fastest download speeds: only a 3.25% loss. That is impressive for a budget option.

The feature that matters most for nomads with multiple devices: unlimited simultaneous connections. Most VPNs cap you at 10 devices. Surfshark does not care. Connect your laptop, phone, tablet, and whatever else you carry, all at once. I have five devices connected right now and performance is fine.

The trade off: renewal prices jump significantly. Most VPNs do this, but Surfshark's jump is notable. CleanWeb ad blocking works well but can be inconsistent across different sites. And their app interface is not as polished as ExpressVPN.

$1.99/month on a 2 year plan. Best value among the four.

---

## ExpressVPN: best if you have never used a VPN before

ExpressVPN has the cleanest app of any VPN I have tested. If you are setting up a VPN for the first time and do not want to think about protocols, kill switches, or split tunneling, get ExpressVPN. It just works, and the interface is genuinely intuitive. I set my mother up with it and she did not call me once for help.

Their Lightway protocol is fast. PCMag showed 18.55% speed loss. Not the fastest, but completely usable. Lightway was designed for switching between WiFi and mobile networks without dropping the connection. For nomads who move constantly between coworking spaces, cafes, and accommodations, this reliability matters more than raw speed.

The downside: fewer advanced features. No double VPN. No multi hop. If you are a power user who wants to configure everything, ExpressVPN will feel limiting. And at $2.79/month, it is not cheap for what you get.

$2.79/month on a 2 year plan. Best for beginners who value simplicity.

## ProtonVPN: best for privacy

If you are a journalist, an activist, or someone who takes digital privacy seriously, ProtonVPN is the one. It is fully open source, based in Switzerland (strong privacy laws), and operated by the same team behind Proton Mail. Every server runs full disk encryption.

The free tier is genuinely useful: unlimited data, no ads. Most free VPNs sell your data. ProtonVPN's free tier is funded by its paid users. If you cannot afford a VPN right now, use ProtonVPN Free instead of downloading something random from an app store. I keep it installed as a backup. The free tier has saved me twice when my primary VPN had an outage.

The downside: speed. PCMag showed a 20% download loss and latency increases notably higher than the competition. Their "VPN Accelerator" feature helps close the gap, but Proton is not the fastest option for streaming or large file transfers. I would not use it for a Netflix night.

Free (unlimited, no ads) or $2.99/month for paid. Best for privacy focused users.

## What about speed?

Here is what PCMag's 2026 tests found: the percentage of speed lost when connected to each VPN.

| | Download loss | Upload loss | Latency increase |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surfshark | 3.25% | 6.07% | 83.92% |
| NordVPN | 5.20% | 5.78% | 58.14% |
| ExpressVPN | 18.55% | 7.45% | 95.62% |
| ProtonVPN | 20.11% | 43.21% | 351.26% |

What this actually means: on a 100 Mbps connection, NordVPN and Surfshark give you about 95 Mbps. ExpressVPN gives you about 81 Mbps. ProtonVPN gives you about 80 Mbps. For 99% of remote work (video calls, file uploads, streaming) any of these is fast enough. The differences only matter for very large file transfers or competitive gaming.

All four offer 30 day money back guarantees. Try one for a month in a country where it matters. If it is not working for you, switch. I have done exactly that twice.

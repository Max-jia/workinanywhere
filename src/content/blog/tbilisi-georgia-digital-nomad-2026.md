Tbilisi sits between Europe and Asia, and in 2026 it has become one of the most talked about digital nomad destinations in the world. Without the crowds and prices of Lisbon or Bali.

I find Georgia's capital unlike any other nomad hub. Here is why remote workers keep choosing it as a long term base.

---

## The visa: 365 days, no questions

Citizens of 95-plus countries (the US, UK, EU, Canada, and Australia included) can stay in Georgia for up to 365 days visa free. No application. No fee. No income proof. You show up at the border with a valid passport, and you are in for a year.

There is also a formal "Remotely from Georgia" program with no minimum income requirement. Just proof of remote work. As of March 2026, new rules require foreign freelancers to register for a work permit and residence permit. Check current requirements before arriving. I have learned the hard way that visa rules in Georgia shift without much notice, so double check even if you read something a few months ago.

Travel medical insurance with at least 30,000 GEL (~$10,800) coverage is mandatory from January 2026.

## The tax system (the real draw)

Georgia has one of the lowest tax burdens in the world for small business owners and freelancers:

| Tax Status | Rate | Turnover Cap |
|------------|------|-------------|
| Micro Business | 0% | 30,000 GEL (~$11,000)/year |
| Small Business Status | 1% | 500,000 GEL (~$180,000)/year |
| Standard IE | 20% on net profit | No cap |
| IT Virtual Zone | 0% corporate tax, 5% on dividends | IT export income only |

Most freelancers register for Small Business Status: 1% tax on gross revenue up to about $180,000 per year. I have not found a more favorable tax setup for remote workers anywhere. Not even close.

Registration is straightforward: visit a Public Service Hall or apply online, get a Tax ID, register as an Individual Entrepreneur, apply for SBS, open a bank account (TBC or Bank of Georgia).

Tax residency triggers at 183-plus days. Once you pass this threshold, you file and pay in Georgia. At these rates, many nomads I know consider that a feature, not a problem.

## Coworking and community

Tbilisi has 30-plus coworking spaces with 90 to 150 Mbps fiber standard. The ones worth knowing:

- Fabrika Coworking: the social hub. Located in a repurposed Soviet sewing factory compound with a courtyard, street food vendors, bars, and a hostel. Best for meeting other nomads.
- Impact Hub Tbilisi: professional, structured. Meeting rooms and fixed desks. Better for quiet focus and client calls.
- Terminal: popular space in a former printing house with a strong community vibe.
- Stamba: part of the Stamba Hotel complex. Stylish, creative atmosphere.

I have worked from Fabrika and Terminal. Fabrika is where you go to meet people. Terminal is where you go to actually work.

## Food and wine

Georgia invented wine 8,000 years ago. A glass of natural qvevri wine costs $2 to $4. The food is outstanding and absurdly affordable:

- Khachapuri (cheese filled bread boat): ~$3
- Khinkali (soup dumplings): ~$0.70 each
- Full dinner with wine: $10 to 15

I still think about the khachapuri at a basement spot in Sololaki whose name I never learned. The cheese pulls were a foot long. Three dollars.

## What to know

Pros:
- 1 year visa free entry for most Western passports
- Extremely favorable tax environment (1% for most freelancers)
- Affordable cost of living with European city infrastructure
- Incredible food and wine culture
- Growing, welcoming nomad community

Cons:
- New 2026 work permit requirements add some bureaucracy
- English is not widely spoken outside the nomad and city center bubble
- Infrastructure (sidewalks, power) can be inconsistent
- Winter is cold (not extreme, but not Mediterranean)
- Direct flights are limited compared to major European hubs

---

I have looked at a lot of nomad destinations, and Tbilisi gets the basics right in a way few places do. Affordability, visa freedom, tax efficiency. All three. The nomad community here is smaller than in Chiang Mai or Lisbon. Depending on what you want, that may be exactly the point. I prefer it that way.

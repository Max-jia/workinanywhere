I first landed in Buenos Aires in early 2024, when the blue dollar was still alive and every nomad I met had a story about walking into a Western Union with a backpack full of cash. I came back in 2026 and the city feels different. The arbitrage game is dead. But I have been here three months this year, and I can tell you exactly what a nomad budget actually looks like now.

---

## The blue dollar is dead

For years, Argentina had a massive gap between the official exchange rate and the informal blue dollar rate. Nomads who brought cash or used Western Union could double their spending power. The 2025 removal of capital controls (*el cepo*) unified the exchange market. The blue dollar premium has collapsed to just 2 to 3 percent.

Your foreign Visa or Mastercard now charges at a near market rate automatically. There is no more cash game. Prices are what they appear to be. The legendary arbitrage advantage that made BA absurdly cheap is gone. When I first arrived in 2024, I remember paying 1,200 pesos for a steak dinner that would have cost $30 at the official rate but only $12 at the blue rate. That math no longer works.

Most furnished rentals in expat areas are quoted and paid in USD. A weaker peso does not help with your single biggest expense. I learned this the hard way. My landlord in Palermo quoted in dollars and would not even discuss pesos.

## The rest is still cheap

Outside of rent, daily life in Buenos Aires is still genuinely cheap. What I actually pay:

- Dinner for two with wine at a good parrilla: $25 to $50
- Cortado (espresso with milk): $1.50 to $3.00
- Subway (Subte) ride: about $0.85
- Beer at a bar: $2 to $4
- Monthly fiber internet (100 to 300 Mbps): $10 to $21

This is what keeps nomads in BA. Once you lock in rent, everything else feels like a bargain compared to North America or Western Europe. I go out most nights, eat at good restaurants regularly, and still spend less than I would on groceries and rent alone in a US city.

## The digital nomad visa

I went through this process in 2025 and it was simpler than I expected:

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Duration | 180 days, renewable once (total 1 year) |
| Income requirement | No fixed minimum. You must show stable foreign income |
| Fee | About $200 |
| Processing | Up to 30 days |
| Tax on foreign income | Generally not taxed for short stays |

For longer stays, the *rentista* route (about $2,000 per month passive income) leads to residency and eventually citizenship.

## Economic context

Inflation has dropped dramatically, from 211% in 2023 to a projected 20% in 2026. The exchange rate system is near unified. Argentina has gone from an economic outlier to something approaching normal. That is good for stability but it also means the days of fire sale prices are over.

The local economy is still strained. The contrast between expat spending power and local wages is sharp. Minimum wage workers have lost about 40 percent of their purchasing power since late 2023. You notice this, and it is worth being mindful of.

---

## Is it still worth it?

I think so, but with adjusted expectations. Buenos Aires still gives you a European style city at roughly half the cost of Western capitals. The food, culture, nightlife, and architecture are world class. Fiber internet is reliable. The nomad community is established and welcoming.

But this is no longer the place where you can live well on $1,000 a month. Budget $1,700 to $2,200 for a comfortable solo lifestyle. That is still excellent value. It just is not the absurd bargain it was in 2020 to 2023.

If you are staying more than a month, skip Airbnb. Short term furnished leases cost 20 to 60 percent less. Ask about paying in pesos. Some landlords will say yes, especially in Villa Crespo or Almagro rather than Palermo. Your biggest savings will always come from rent, not from daily spending.

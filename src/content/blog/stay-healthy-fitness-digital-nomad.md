Staying healthy as a digital nomad is harder than it looks. Your gym, your grocery store, your doctor, and your routine all reset every time you move. The habits that kept you healthy at home do not automatically survive the lifestyle.

I have been a nomad for several years, and I have tried just about every fitness hack, nutrition plan, and sleep gadget that fits in a carry-on. Most of them are useless. This is what has actually worked for me.

---

## Fitness: you do not need a gym

The best fitness strategy for nomads is to stop depending on gyms. When every new city means finding, touring, and signing up for a new gym, most people simply stop going. Bodyweight workouts require zero equipment, work anywhere, and are always available. I have not had a gym membership since 2023. I am in better shape now than when I did.

### The core bodyweight routine (25 to 35 minutes)

3 rounds, 60 seconds rest between rounds:

| Exercise | Reps |
|----------|------|
| Push-ups (standard or elevated feet) | 15 to 20 |
| Bodyweight squats | 20 |
| Reverse lunges | 12 each leg |
| Plank | 45 seconds |
| Glute bridges | 20 |
| Tricep dips (on a chair or bed) | 15 |
| Mountain climbers | 30 seconds |

Add a 4th round when 3 rounds become comfortable. Slow the tempo for more intensity. I do this routine three times a week in whatever apartment or park I find myself in. It has outlasted every gym phase I ever had.

### Minimalist gear worth packing

- **Resistance bands:** Hundreds of exercise variations, weigh almost nothing
- **Jump rope:** Compact, excellent cardio
- **Running shoes:** Double for walking and light hiking

### What about walking?

A day of exploring a new city covering 8,000 to 15,000 steps is real physical activity. On heavy walking days, do not feel like you also need a full workout. Walking counts. In Tokyo last year, I averaged 18,000 steps a day and skipped workouts entirely for two weeks. I came back fitter than before.

## Nutrition: simple anchors in an unstable environment

You cannot control every meal as a nomad, but you can control a few anchors that keep your nutrition from falling apart:

1. **Prioritize protein at every meal.** Eggs, legumes, grilled meats, local fish, Greek yogurt. Protein keeps you full and supports muscle maintenance.
2. **Eat local.** Market food and street food are typically fresher, cheaper, and more nutritious than tourist-facing restaurants. The best meal I had in 2025 cost $1.50 at a market stall in Da Nang.
3. **One restaurant meal per day.** Restaurant food is usually richer than what you would cook at home. Cook or buy fresh produce for your other meals when possible.
4. **Hydrate deliberately.** Carry a filtered water bottle. Start every day with a full glass of water. Dehydration wrecks your focus faster than most people realize.
5. **Limit alcohol to 1 to 2 drinks.** I am not saying eliminate it. Just keep it moderate to preserve sleep quality and recovery. I learned this after a month in Mexico City where I said yes to every mezcal offered. I was functional but fuzzy the entire time.

## Sleep: the foundation

Sleep is the first thing that suffers when you are excited about a new city. When sleep suffers, everything else gets harder.

- **Portable sleep kit:** Eye mask, foam earplugs, white noise app. I use all three every night.
- **Jet lag:** Get bright natural light within one hour of waking. Shift to local time immediately. This one rule cuts my jet lag recovery time in half.
- **Before sleep ritual:** Same stretches, same music, same sequence. A routine you can repeat anywhere tells your brain it is time to wind down.

## Mental health

The biggest health challenge for many nomads is not physical. It is mental. Constant movement, social resets, and isolation take a toll that is easy to dismiss until it gets severe.

- **Set a weekly social minimum.** At least 2 meaningful interactions beyond transactions. Coworking spaces, language exchanges, and scheduled calls with old friends all count.
- **Morning mindfulness.** 5 minutes of breathwork or meditation reduces cortisol levels.
- **Know the signs.** If you are avoiding social situations, romanticizing your old office job, or feeling relief when trips get canceled, slow down.
- **Professional help is accessible.** Many therapists now specialize in expat and nomad mental health. Online platforms make sessions available from anywhere with an internet connection.

## Healthcare: how to find a doctor abroad

- **Insurance first.** Get coverage before you need it. SafetyWing ($56 to $68 per month) for basic travel insurance. Genki ($75 to $90 per month) for more comprehensive health coverage including routine care.
- **Know where to go before you are sick.** In each new city, identify the nearest English speaking clinic or hospital. Do not wait until you have a high fever to start searching.
- **Carry a basic first aid kit:** painkillers, antihistamines, antidiarrheal medication, band aids, antiseptic cream. These are the things you need at 2am, not things you want to hunt for in an unfamiliar pharmacy.
- **Prescriptions:** Bring enough for your entire trip duration plus copies of your prescriptions. Some medications available over the counter in one country require a prescription in another. I once spent a full day in Greece trying to get a prescription refilled that was OTC back home. Plan ahead.

---

I do not aim for perfection with any of this. A 10-minute workout is far better than skipping because you could not do a full hour. One healthy meal a day is better than none. Five minutes of mindfulness is better than nothing. The nomads who stay healthy long term are the ones who maintain the habit, not the ones who maintain the perfect routine. I am not the fittest nomad I know. I am just still going, which is its own kind of win.

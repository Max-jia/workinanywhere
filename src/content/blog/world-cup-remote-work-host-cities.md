The World Cup is here. 104 matches across 16 cities in the US, Canada, and Mexico. The crowds are massive, the energy is absurd, and if you are a remote worker who has ever thought *"can I just... go work from there for a few weeks?"* you are not alone.

Digital nomads are flooding host cities. Not just for match day. They are arriving weeks early, settling into coworking spaces, and treating the World Cup as the world's biggest workcation. Euronews called it *"the largest temporary remote work experiment in sports history."*

The trend pieces skip one problem: not all host cities are built for this. Some have 266 coworking spaces within reach. Others have 13. Some cities offer affordable hotels near stadiums. Others will charge you $4,000 a week for a room within walking distance of a match.

I dug through the data from 20i's host city rankings, coworking availability maps, and cost of living comparisons. Here is where you actually want to set up your laptop during the World Cup.

---

## The winner: Toronto (200/250)

If you can only pick one city to work from during this World Cup, pick Toronto.

The internet is blazing (200+ Mbps). There are 113 coworking spaces downtown. Canada's digital nomad policy lets remote workers with foreign employers stay up to 6 months without a visa, and potentially convert to a work permit. The time zone overlaps with Europe for about 3 hours each morning, so you can get your meetings done, watch a match in the afternoon, and still make happy hour.

BMO Field is not the biggest stadium, but Toronto's infrastructure is the best of any host city. The city expects $3.8 billion in economic impact during the tournament. The coworking scene has been preparing for this. I would pick Toronto over any US host city without hesitation.

The catch: Canada is not cheap. Budget $110 to 140 CAD per day. But you are paying for reliability, not scenery.

---

## The dark horse: Houston (146.7/250)

Houston was not on my radar until I looked at the numbers. Now I cannot stop recommending it.

$1.25 transit (cheapest in the US). 73 coworking spaces. 220 affordable accommodation options near the stadium. 178 Mbps average broadband. A day pass at a coworking desk runs about $30, the cheapest among major US host cities. A meal out costs about $20. A beer? Six bucks.

NRG Stadium is an urban venue. You are not stuck in suburban sprawl when the match ends. There are 16 coworking spaces within 3 miles. This is the sleeper pick for someone who wants to work, watch football, and not drain their bank account.

---

## For the coworking obsessed: New York / New Jersey

266 coworking spaces. More than any other host city by a wide margin. I triple checked this number because it seemed wrong. It is not wrong.

Work from Manhattan or Brooklyn during the week. Hop a train to MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford on match day. The density of cafes with reliable WiFi is absurd. If your idea of a good workcation is being surrounded by other people who are also working, this is your city.

The downside: you will pay for it. Meals run $25 to 35, beers $8 to 11. Accommodation near the stadium is scarce. Do not try to stay in East Rutherford. Stay in the city, commute to the match, and treat the workday as the main event.

---

## The budget pick: Mexico City

Mexico City is the cheapest host city by a significant margin. $3 beers. $12 meals. Taxis at $0.46 per kilometer. Your daily budget drops to $45 to 65, roughly a third of what you would spend in a US host city.

There are 171 coworking spaces in the city. The digital nomad community is established and welcoming. The 180 day tourist visa for many passports means zero paperwork. I have worked from CDMX twice and the coworking scene punches well above its cost.

The internet catch: average speed is only 43.6 Mbps. Bring a backup connection and a VPN. Cybersecurity scores in Mexico sit well below the 80 point "good" benchmark. This is not a dealbreaker, but it means you need to be more deliberate about where and how you work. Do not handle sensitive client data on cafe WiFi. Use your coworking space's wired connection. A VPN is not optional here.

---

## The European time zone play: Philadelphia & Atlanta

If you work with a European team, the US East Coast gives you roughly 3 hours of overlap with London every morning. Philadelphia and Atlanta both have urban stadiums. Mercedes-Benz Stadium in Atlanta has 53 coworking spaces within 3 miles. Philadelphia's day pass median is $32, the lowest in the US.

Both cities have decent internet (175+ Mbps) and reasonable accommodation availability. Philly has 465 affordable options near the stadium. If you are juggling morning meetings with afternoon matches, the East Coast time zone is your friend.

---

## The wild cards: Vancouver & Guadalajara

Vancouver has the second best overall score (163/250) and internet speeds above 200 Mbps. The scenery is absurd. The Canada digital nomad policy applies here too. But time zone alignment with Europe is terrible: zero overlap. If you are an independent freelancer who does not need real time collaboration, this is your spot.

Guadalajara is Mexico's dark horse. It is hosting 13 matches and has seen a surge in digital nomad interest, especially from Spanish travelers. Accommodation is more affordable than Mexico City, and the local government has been actively courting remote workers. Same internet caveats as Mexico City apply: bring a VPN, plan your workspace carefully.

---

## Three things nobody tells you about working during the World Cup

1. Do not stay near the stadium. Urban stadiums (Atlanta, Houston, Philly, Seattle) are walkable from downtown coworking zones. Suburban stadiums (Gillette in Boston, MetLife in NY, AT&T in Dallas, SoFi in LA) sit in the middle of nowhere. Stay downtown and commute to the match. Your workday will thank you.

2. Book coworking day passes in advance. Spaces in host cities are filling up. Do not assume you can walk in on match day and find a desk. Book ahead, even if it is just a day pass. I watched someone get turned away from three spaces in Mexico City on a group stage match day. Do not be that person.

3. The real World Cup experience is not at the stadium. It is in the bars, the watch parties, the streets after a big win. Do not blow your entire budget on match tickets. Some of the best memories happen at a coworking space watch party with people you met three days ago.

FlexJobs released their 2026 digital nomad report last month, and one number stopped me: 36% of Americans said they would move to a new city, state, or country if they had a work from anywhere job. Another 17% said they would travel frequently as a digital nomad. That is more than half the workforce.

The demand is there. The question is whether the jobs are. I went through FlexJobs' data from January to May 2026 to find out who is actually hiring.

---

## The top 10 work from anywhere companies

These companies had the most work from anywhere job listings in the first half of 2026:

1. **Wikimedia Foundation** — The nonprofit behind Wikipedia. Fully distributed, async first culture. If your idea of meaningful work involves making knowledge free, this is it.
2. **Xapo Bank** — Crypto native bank. Fully remote, hires globally. Competitive comp, unusual for a financial services company.
3. **Supabase** — Open source Firebase alternative. Remote first, engineering heavy, transparent culture. Their CEO tweets about company metrics publicly.
4. **Railway** — Platform as a service startup. Fast growing, remote first, appeals to developers who like infrastructure.
5. **Eliassen Group** — IT staffing and consulting. Less flashy than the startups on this list, but stable and consistently hires remote roles.
6. **G2i** — Developer talent platform. Work from anywhere roles for engineers, focused on matching talent with startups.
7. **Invisible Technologies** — Operations and process automation. Quietly one of the largest fully remote companies. Hires globally across operations, engineering, and leadership.
8. **Superside** — Creative as a service platform. Fully remote creative teams. Designers, project managers, and account roles.
9. **Cloudlinux** — Enterprise Linux OS. Remote first, hires globally for engineering and support roles.
10. **Appinio** — Market research platform. Remote across Europe and the Americas.

## The hottest roles right now

FlexJobs tracked the most in demand remote positions. If you are wondering what to skill up in, start here:

- Customer Success Manager
- Executive Assistant
- Data Scientist
- Software Engineer
- Social Media Manager
- Operations Manager
- Account Manager
- Visual Designer
- Data Engineer
- Product Manager

The range is wider than most people think. It is not just developers. Companies need people who can write, manage, design, and communicate. They increasingly do not care where those people sit.

I have hired remote workers across three of these categories, and the quality of applicants who understand async work versus those who treat remote like "office at home" is night and day. The companies on this list know the difference.

---

## What this means for your job hunt

The competition for remote jobs in 2026 is genuinely different from 2020. Companies are not scrambling to figure out remote work anymore. They have figured it out. The ones that committed to work from anywhere did so deliberately, and they filter for people who already know how to work remotely.

I have applied to remote roles at three of these companies. The application processes reward people who understand distributed work. If your cover letter talks about collaborating across time zones instead of being a team player, you are already ahead of most applicants.

Demonstrate remote competence in your application. Mention your home office setup. Your experience with async tools. Your time zone flexibility. Do not make a hiring manager guess whether you can handle remote work. Show them you already have.

The companies on this list are not doing you a favor by letting you work remotely. They built their entire operations around distributed teams. Apply like you understand that.

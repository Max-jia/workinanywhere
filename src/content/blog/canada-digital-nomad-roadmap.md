Southeast Asian countries fight over digital nomad visa requirements. Canada built something simpler: a policy that lets remote workers with foreign employers stay for up to six months without a visa, with a path to convert to a work permit if they want to stay longer.

The policy launched in 2023 and has not gotten the attention it deserves. The World Cup is changing that. Toronto and Vancouver are hosting matches, and nomads arriving for the tournament are discovering a policy that makes long term stays surprisingly easy. I spent three months in Toronto last year under this exact setup, and border control barely blinked.

---

## What the policy actually says

If you work remotely for a foreign employer, you can enter Canada as a visitor and stay for up to six months. No visa application. No income threshold. No proof of savings. You need to show that your work is for a company outside Canada and that you can support yourself during your stay. That is the whole framework.

The important thing to understand: you enter as a visitor who happens to work remotely. Canada does not have a "digital nomad visa." This means you follow visitor rules, not work permit rules. You cannot work for Canadian companies or Canadian clients. Your income must come from outside the country.

The six months can become a work permit if you get a job offer from a Canadian employer during your stay. That is the roadmap part: arrive as a visitor, experience the country, and if you find an opportunity to contribute to the Canadian economy, a path to stay longer exists. I have not tested this myself, but I met two people in Toronto who went from tourist to work permit holder in under four months.

## Why it matters right now

Toronto scored 200 out of 250 points in the World Cup remote work rankings, the highest of any host city. Vancouver was second at 163. The infrastructure backs up the numbers: 200+ Mbps broadband, dense coworking networks, strong cybersecurity, and time zones that overlap with European working hours.

During the World Cup, Toronto alone expects $3.8 billion in economic impact. Digital nomads are part of that number, through tourism spending and through extended stays that involve coworking memberships, apartment rentals, and integration into local business communities.

---

## Toronto vs. Vancouver

Both cities operate under the same national policy. The experience differs.

Toronto is the business hub. More coworking spaces, better transit, more diverse food scene, more expensive accommodation. If you want to be in the center of things, especially during the World Cup, Toronto is the pick. I prefer it for the energy alone.

Vancouver is the lifestyle choice. Better scenery, more outdoor access, slightly better internet speeds, slightly worse time zone alignment with Europe. If you value nature and a slower pace, Vancouver wins.

Both are expensive by global nomad standards. Budget $110 to 140 CAD per day in either city. This is not a budget destination. It is a quality of life destination for people who can afford it. You need to be honest with yourself about whether that price tag works for you.

## What to know before you go

Banking: open a Canadian bank account if you are staying more than a couple of months. Wise and Revolut work, but local banking makes life easier for rent payments and daily transactions. I used Wise for two months before opening an account with TD, and the difference in friction was noticeable.

Healthcare: Canada's healthcare system does not cover visitors. You need travel health insurance. SafetyWing, World Nomads, and Genki all cover Canada. Do not skip this. Out of pocket medical costs in Canada are significant. A friend of mine skipped insurance and paid $1,200 for a single urgent care visit.

Accommodation: Airbnb works for short stays. For anything over a month, use local rental platforms or negotiate directly with landlords. Furnished Finder, originally built for traveling nurses, has expanded into Canada and is worth checking.

Coworking: book ahead, especially in Toronto. World Cup demand is filling spaces. Day passes run $30 to 40 CAD. Monthly memberships are better value if you are staying three weeks or more.

---

## Is Canada right for you?

Canada is a strong option if you want North American infrastructure with a more welcoming policy environment than the US offers, and you can afford the cost of living. It is the wrong option if you are budget constrained or looking for a tropical beach lifestyle.

The six month policy is useful. The work permit pathway is a real option for people who want to stay. If you are already planning to be in North America for the World Cup, this is the moment to use the policy. I would pick it over most Southeast Asian options right now, and I say that as someone who spent a year bouncing between Chiang Mai and Bali.

Every budget guide for digital nomads starts with the same line: your spending depends on your lifestyle. True and useless. If you found this article, you want to know whether $1,000 a month actually works somewhere worth being.

It does. I have done it. It is not glamorous. You eat mostly local food. You live in a studio, not a one bedroom with a balcony. You skip the imported cheese and the craft cocktails. You have enough. You do not have luxuries. But you also do not have that low-grade anxiety of watching your bank balance shrink every week.

These five cities are ones where I know the math works, either from living there myself or from watching friends do it.

---

## Da Nang, Vietnam: $700 to $1,000

Da Nang is the best value beach city in Southeast Asia and I will die on that hill. A modern studio near the beach costs $350 to $500. Street food is $1 to $3 a meal. A coworking membership at ACE or Enouvo runs $60 to $90 a month. A local SIM with 50GB of data costs about $5. I remember paying $1.20 for a banh mi and a coffee and thinking the math had to be wrong.

The city is clean, modern, and safe. The beach stretches for miles. The internet is reliable. The nomad community is small but growing. You will not find the density of Chiang Mai or Bali here, which is either good or bad depending on what you want. At this budget, you can eat well, work productively, and still afford a weekend trip to Hoi An without sweating the numbers.

---

## Chiang Mai, Thailand: $700 to $950

Chiang Mai on $700 a month is tight but doable. I spent four months here a few years back and kept my spending under $800 without trying hard. A studio in Santitham costs $200 to $300. Street food is $1 to $2 a meal. Coworking spaces on Nimmanhaemin Road cost $70 to $120 a month. The biggest variable is whether you rent a scooter or use Grab. The scooter is cheaper over a month. Grab is safer. I chose Grab.

At $950, you have breathing room. You can eat at restaurants a few times a week, join a gym, and take a weekend trip to Pai. Chiang Mai rewards frugality better than almost any other city on earth. The infrastructure is good. The community is massive. The food lives up to the hype. You just have to accept that burning season runs February through April and plan to leave during those months. Nobody stays through burning season twice.

---

## Tbilisi, Georgia: $800 to $1,000

Tbilisi is the most underrated budget nomad destination in Europe and I will keep saying it until more people catch on. Most Western passport holders get 365 days visa free on arrival. A one bedroom apartment in a central neighborhood like Vera or Marjanishvili costs $400 to $600. A meal at a local restaurant is $5 to $8. Georgian wine, which the country has been making for 8,000 years, costs $2 to $4 a glass.

The internet is fast. The coworking scene is growing. The food is outstanding. The tax system is extraordinarily friendly to freelancers: 1% tax on gross revenue up to about $180,000 a year. Tbilisi is not a polished European capital. It is a city with rough edges, incredible hospitality, and a cost of living that makes Western nomads feel wealthy. If you want character over comfort, this is your place.

---

## Penang, Malaysia: $800 to $1,000

Penang is the food destination that digital nomads keep discovering and nobody talks about loudly, because they want to keep it that way. A studio apartment in George Town costs $300 to $500. Street food is legendary and costs $1 to $3 per meal. The coworking scene is small but functional. The island has a laid back pace that makes you wonder why you ever rushed through life.

Malaysia's DE Rantau Nomad Pass gives you a legal framework for stays of three to twelve months. The income threshold is lower than most nomad visas: around $24,000 a year for tech workers. English is widely spoken. The infrastructure is excellent. Penang combines the food culture of Southeast Asia with the infrastructure of a developed country, at a price that makes both feel like a bargain. I have not lived in Penang myself, but three friends have spent six-plus months there and none of them want to leave.

---

## Sarajevo, Bosnia: $800 to $1,000

Sarajevo is the cheapest city in Europe worth living in. A studio apartment in the city center costs $300 to $450. A meal at a local restaurant is $4 to $7. Coffee culture is serious here: the city was making espresso before most of Europe caught on. The internet is reliable. The coworking scene is small but the cafe scene is excellent.

Bosnia is not in the Schengen Area, which matters for visa planning. You can spend 90 days here without burning your Schengen days. The city carries the weight of its history. It is not a party destination or a beach destination. It is a place where you can work deeply, live cheaply, and feel like you are somewhere that matters. A friend of mine lived there for six months and told me he wrote more in Sarajevo than anywhere else. I believe him.

## The trade-off at $1,000

At $1,000 a month, you trade choice for value. You cannot pick any city in the world. You pick from a specific list of cities where the math adds up. You eat local food because imported groceries eat your budget. You rent studios because one bedrooms eat your budget. You fly budget airlines because premium carriers eat your budget.

The people who burn out at this budget level are the ones trying to live a $2,000 lifestyle on $1,000. I learned this the hard way in my first year. You accept the constraints or you go broke pretending they do not exist. The constraints are not the enemy. They are what make it possible.

## FAQ

**Which is the absolute cheapest city for digital nomads?**
Da Nang, Vietnam. You can live comfortably on $650 to $900 a month. A studio apartment costs $300 to $450. Local meals are $1 to $3. The beach is free. The main trade-off is the visa situation: Vietnam does not have a long term digital nomad visa, so most people do 90-day tourist visas or monthly border runs.

**Is the internet reliable in these cities?**
Generally yes. Chiang Mai and Da Nang have 50 to 200 Mbps connections widely available. Tbilisi has solid 30 to 100 Mbps. Sarajevo is the weakest of the five, but cafes and coworking spaces have reliable connections. If your work depends on video calls, Chiang Mai and Da Nang are the safest bets. See our [full comparison of best digital nomad cities](/blog/best-digital-nomad-destinations-compared-2026) for internet speed data.

**Do I need a visa for these cities?**
It depends on your passport. Most Western passport holders get 30 to 90 days visa free in Thailand, Vietnam, Georgia, and Bosnia. Georgia offers one year visa free entry. If you plan to stay longer than 90 days, check the [specific digital nomad visa for your target country](/blog/50-countries-nomad-visas-2026).

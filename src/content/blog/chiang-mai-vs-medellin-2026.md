I have lived in both cities. Six months in Chiang Mai, four in Medellin. They are the two mid-budget nomad hubs everyone recommends, and on paper they look similar. Both have solid communities, cheap food, and enough coworking spaces to keep you busy.

But they are completely different experiences. Pick the wrong one for your personality and you will be on a flight out within a month. I almost was. This is what I learned from living in both places.

---

## The feel of each city

Chiang Mai is calm. Temples, street food, a slower rhythm that made me wonder why I ever rushed through life. My mornings started with a $1.50 khao soi from a street stall on Nimmanhaemin Road. Afternoons at a coworking space. Evenings at the night markets. The city wraps around you. It is comfortable. Sometimes too comfortable. After a few months, I got restless.

Medellin is energy. Salsa music spills from open windows. Every cafe hums with Spanish conversation. Green mountains frame the skyline and every time I looked up, it caught me off guard. You feel more alive here. If Chiang Mai is a warm blanket, Medellin is a shot of espresso.

Neither city is better in some abstract sense. But I prefer Medellin, and I think most people who read this will too once they understand the tradeoffs.

---

## What your money gets you

| | Chiang Mai | Medellin |
|---|---|---|
| Studio/1-bed | $250-400 | $500-750 |
| Street food meal | $1-3 | $3-6 |
| Coworking monthly | $70-150 | $80-200 |
| Monthly total (comfortable) | $900-1,200 | $1,200-1,600 |

Chiang Mai wins on pure cost. Your money stretches further here than almost anywhere with comparable infrastructure. But cheaper is not the same as better value. Medellin costs more and in my experience it is worth the premium. Better weather, easier time zones for North American clients, a more social environment. If you have the budget, spend it on Medellin.

---

## The climate (this is the real difference)

This is where these cities split hardest, and it is the reason many people flee one for the other.

Medellin has near-perfect weather. 63 to 83 degrees Fahrenheit, year round. No AC. No heating. Open your windows and you are comfortable every single day. They call it the "City of Eternal Spring" and for once the nickname is accurate.

Chiang Mai is hot. Actually hot, especially March through June. And then there is burning season. From February to April, agricultural fires blanket northern Thailand in hazardous smog. I cannot overstate how bad this gets. Masks. Air purifiers running 24/7. I left during burning season both years and so does everyone who knows what is coming.

If you have seasonal flexibility and do not mind heat, Chiang Mai's lower cost makes it worth planning around burning season. If you want consistent comfortable weather without thinking about it, Medellin wins by a mile.

---

## Community and social life

Chiang Mai has the larger nomad community. About 20,000 remote workers at any given time. It is easy to meet people. The pace is social but relaxed: coworking during the day, night markets and street food crawls in the evening. English is widely spoken in nomad areas. I found the Chiang Mai community easier to enter as a newcomer.

Medellin has about 11,400 nomads. Smaller crowd, more energy. The nightlife is livelier. If you want to go out dancing on a Tuesday, Medellin has it. But English is less common outside the expat bubble. You need basic Spanish to feel comfortable in daily life. I struggled with this the first few weeks. Ordering coffee in broken Spanish is humbling.

Pick Chiang Mai if you want a calm, friendly community and an easy social experience. Pick Medellin if you want nightlife, energy, and are willing to learn some Spanish. I found both communities welcoming, just in different ways.

---

## Safety

Chiang Mai is one of the safest cities on any nomad list. Violent crime against foreigners is extremely rare. Your biggest risk is a traffic accident on a motorbike. I walked home alone at 2am more times than I can count and never felt unsafe.

Medellin is trickier. El Poblado and Laureles are safe if you follow basic precautions. Outside those zones, the situation changes fast. Phone snatching and petty theft happen. I know three people who had their phones grabbed in Medellin. You need situational awareness. Do not flash valuables. Do not walk alone late at night in unfamiliar areas. Use Uber, never hail a street taxi. Plenty of nomads live in Medellin for years without trouble. Plenty of others lose their phone on day three. Both are true.

## Visas

| | Chiang Mai | Medellin |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term visa | DTV: 5 years, 180-day stays | DN Visa: 2 years |
| Key requirement | 500,000 THB savings (~$14,500) | ~$1,450/month income |
| Income proof needed? | No | Yes (3 months, no averaging) |
| Fee | ~$280-400 | ~$75 |

Medellin's visa has a lower financial barrier and a nomad-specific framework. Chiang Mai's DTV is more flexible long term but requires real savings in the bank. If you do not have $14,500 liquid, Medellin is the easier door to walk through.

---

## Time zone (this matters more than you think)

Chiang Mai is UTC+7. That is 12 hours ahead of US East Coast. If you have regular meetings with American clients or teammates, you are working nights. I tried it for two months. By week three I was miserable.

Medellin is UTC-5. Same as US Eastern time. Your workday lines up naturally with North American schedules. For anyone working with US or Canadian clients, this alone can tip the decision.

For European clients, the gap is smaller and Chiang Mai's time zone works fine. I worked with a UK-based team from Chiang Mai and the 7-hour difference was manageable.

---

## Which one should you choose?

Go to Chiang Mai if:
- You are budget-conscious or just starting out
- You value calm, safety, and community depth
- Food is a major priority (Chiang Mai's food scene is world class)
- You work with Asia-Pacific or European clients
- You can leave during burning season (February to April)

Go to Medellin if:
- You work with North American clients and need time zone overlap
- Perfect weather is non-negotiable for you
- You want an energetic, social environment
- You speak or are excited to learn Spanish
- You can budget $1,200 or more per month

Or do what I did: spend 3 to 4 months in each. Chiang Mai during the good season (May to January), Medellin during burning season (February to April). Follow the weather. But if you forced me to pick one place to live year round, I would choose Medellin. The weather, the time zone, the energy. Chiang Mai feels like a retreat. Medellin feels like a life.

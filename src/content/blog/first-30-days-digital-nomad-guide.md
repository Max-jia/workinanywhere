The first month as a digital nomad is disorienting. You left your old structure behind and have not built a new one yet. Everything is exciting and overwhelming at once.

I remember my first week in Chiang Mai, standing in a 7-Eleven holding three different SIM cards and wondering why nobody had written down the order of operations for this whole thing. Five years later, this is the timeline I have pieced together. The things that matter, in the order they matter.

---

## Before you leave (week 0)

### Finances
- Set up a Wise or Revolut account for cross border banking
- Get a credit card with no foreign transaction fees
- Tell your bank you will be traveling (frozen cards are a preventable crisis)
- Set aside an emergency fund of at least 2 months of expenses

### Insurance
- Buy travel or nomad health insurance before departure
- Save your policy number offline on your phone

### Documents
- Digital copies of passport, visa, insurance, vaccination records on encrypted cloud storage
- Printed passport copies stored separately from your actual passport
- Extra passport photos (some visas require physical copies)

### Packing
- Pack for 7 to 10 days, not 30. You can do laundry.
- Electronics: laptop, noise canceling headphones, unlocked phone, universal power adapter, power bank
- Merino wool clothing if you can afford it. It resists odor, dries fast, and wears longer between washes.
- If you are not sure whether to bring something, do not. You can buy almost anything locally.

## Day 1 to 3: Land and stabilize

Your only jobs in the first three days:

1. Get a local SIM card or activate an eSIM. Data is infrastructure. Do this before you leave the airport or right after you land.
2. Find your accommodation and unpack. Do not try to be productive on arrival day. Settle in. Find the nearest grocery store, pharmacy, and coffee shop.
3. Walk your neighborhood. Orient yourself. What is nearby? Where is the nearest coworking space? Which cafes look laptop friendly? Walking also helps with jet lag.
4. Set up your workspace. Test the WiFi. Find a desk or table that works. If it does not, find the nearest coworking space and buy a day pass for tomorrow.

---

## Day 4 to 7: Build the basics

1. Establish a morning routine. Keep it simple and location independent: wake up, water, walk (15 to 30 minutes, no phone), first work session. The specifics can shift, but the sequence stays the same.
2. Get your first real work done. Ship something. The anxiety of "can I actually be productive here?" disappears the moment you deliver.
3. Find your coworking space. Try 2 or 3 spaces with day passes before committing to a monthly membership. The vibe matters more than the amenities.
4. Join the local nomad groups. Search "[city] digital nomads" or "[city] expats" on WhatsApp, Telegram, and Facebook. Post: "Just arrived, staying for [X months], working in [field]. Anyone around for coffee?" This is normal. You will not look desperate. Every other newcomer is wondering the same thing.
5. Buy a local SIM if you have not already. eSIMs are fine for arrival, but local SIMs are cheaper for longer stays.

---

## Week 2: Go deeper

1. Say yes to everything social for one week. Coworking event that sounds boring? Go. Group dinner with people you just met? Go. The window for integrating into a social circle is short. Front load your social effort.
2. Find your third place. Not home, not work. A cafe, park, or bar you can return to regularly. Familiarity is grounding when everything else is new.
3. Explore beyond the tourist zone. Go to a neighborhood where locals actually live. Eat somewhere without an English menu. This is where you figure out if you actually like a city.
4. Start tracking your spending. The first month is always more expensive than expected (setup costs, SIM cards, deposits, first grocery runs). Track everything so you know what your real monthly run rate looks like.

---

## Week 3 to 4: Lock in

1. Evaluate your workspace. Is the coworking space working? Are you productive in your accommodation? Adjust now. Do not spend three months in a setup that is not serving you.
2. Build a social routine. Find 1 or 2 recurring events (weekly coworking drinks, a language exchange, a sports group) and attend them every week. Familiarity builds connection.
3. Plan your next move, but do not rush. If you are enjoying where you are, stay longer. The slowmad pace (2 to 3 months per location) is more sustainable and socially rewarding than moving every 2 to 3 weeks.
4. Reflect. What is working? What is harder than you expected? Adjust your approach for the next destination based on what you learned.

---

## Common first month mistakes

1. Moving too fast. Trying to see 5 cities in 30 days. You will be exhausted and will not genuinely experience any of them. Pick one place. Stay.
2. Over-planning activities and under-planning work. It is tempting to treat your first month like a vacation. It is not. Protect your work hours.
3. Not joining groups before arriving. Showing up alone and trying to meet people from scratch is the hardest way to do it. Join the groups. Post the message.
4. Comparing your real life to everyone else's Instagram. People post rooftop pools, not the Tuesday night you spent alone wondering if this was a mistake. Both are real. The first month is hard for almost everyone.

---

The first 30 days are about stabilization, not optimization. Land. Get connected. Ship some work. Meet a few people. Everything else can wait for month two.

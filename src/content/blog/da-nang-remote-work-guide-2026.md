I first landed in Da Nang in early 2025, expecting another overhyped nomad spot. I was wrong. The city has first world infrastructure at emerging market prices, and the beach, mountains, and city center all sit within 15 minutes of each other.

This guide covers what I learned after six months of living and working there.

---

## Why Da Nang?

Chiang Mai has no beach. Bali has traffic and an influencer problem. Da Nang is a clean, modern city with internet that works, a beach that stretches for miles, and prices that leave plenty of room in a Western budget. It is less crowded than Bangkok, cheaper than Bali, and the air quality does not force you to flee for three months a year.

The nomad community here is smaller than Chiang Mai or Bali. That is either a pro or a con, depending on what you want. If you want a massive social scene, Chiang Mai wins. If you want a quieter, more grounded daily life with everything you need, Da Nang delivers.

## Best neighborhoods

| Area | Rent (1-Bed) | Vibe |
|------|-------------|------|
| **An Thuong / My An** | $400 to 700 | The nomad hub. Walkable to the beach, packed with cafes, expat friendly |
| **My Khe Beachfront** | $500 to 800 | Best sunsets, more touristy, pricier |
| **Hai Chau (city center)** | $350 to 550 | Local atmosphere, cheaper, near markets |
| **Son Tra Peninsula** | $450 to 650 | Quiet, nature, need a scooter |

Most nomads settle in An Thuong. It is the most walkable neighborhood, the easiest place to get by in English, and has the highest concentration of laptop friendly cafes and coworking spaces.

## Internet & coworking

Da Nang's internet is excellent. Fiber connections of 50 to 150 Mbps are standard in apartments ($10 to 15/month). Mobile data is absurdly cheap: about $5 to 10/month for 50GB via Viettel or Mobifone. 5G is expanding rapidly.

**Real speed test results from nomad frequented cafes:**
- Passport Coffee Club: 351 Mbps download
- Kim Coffee Garden: 233 Mbps
- The Backpacker Hotel: 472 Mbps
- Cafe Nia: 57 Mbps (fine for email, not for video calls)

**Coworking options:**

| Space | Monthly | Notes |
|-------|---------|-------|
| ACE Coworking | ~$90 | Best all around community vibe |
| Enouvo Space (Enosta) | ~$100 | Established nomad hub |
| Toong Da Nang | $80 to 120 | Most professional, multiple locations |
| Dreamplex | ~$10/day | Rooftop, near the beach |

Many nomads also work productively from cafes. Most are laptop friendly, have free WiFi, and do not enforce time limits during off peak hours.

## Visa

Vietnam doesn't have a dedicated digital nomad visa, but options exist:

- **E-visa (90 days):** Single entry ($25) or multiple entry ($50). Apply via evisa.gov.vn. This is what most nomads use.
- **Visa-free (45 days):** Available for many EU and Asian passport holders. Not available for US, Canada, or Australia. Those passports need an e-visa.
- **Business visa (3 to 12 months):** Requires local sponsorship. $150 to 350.
- **Border runs:** Common practice but increasingly scrutinized. Not recommended as a long-term strategy.

## What to know before you go

**Pros:**
- Exceptional value. Your money goes further here than almost anywhere else with comparable infrastructure
- Clean, safe, modern city with excellent roads and public spaces
- Beach, mountains, and city, all accessible
- Food is outstanding and cheap. Vietnamese cuisine is world class
- Reliable, fast internet

**Cons:**
- Rainy season (September to December) brings heavy downpours and occasional flooding
- English is not widely spoken outside tourist and expat areas. Google Translate helps
- Construction noise is common. The city is still growing fast
- No dedicated digital nomad visa. Border runs and e-visa renewals require planning
- Tap water is not drinkable. You will buy bottled or filtered water

---

## Best time to go

**February to August** is the sweet spot: warm, dry, and sunny. September through December is the rainy season, with typhoon risk and occasional flooding. January is transitional.

---

I left Da Nang in October, just as the rains started. I miss the morning swims, the $1.50 banh mi, and the quiet afternoons at Passport Coffee Club where the wifi hits 350 Mbps and nobody bothers you. If you want a beach city where $1,200 to $1,500 a month buys a comfortable life with excellent infrastructure, Da Nang is hard to beat. The nomad community is smaller than in Chiang Mai or Bali, and for a lot of people, that is the point.

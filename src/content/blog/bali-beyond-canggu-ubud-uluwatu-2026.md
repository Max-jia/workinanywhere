Canggu is the default answer when someone says they are working remotely from Bali. It has the highest concentration of coworking spaces, cafes, and nomads on the island. But it is also crowded, increasingly expensive, and not everyone's idea of paradise. The traffic alone has driven many nomads, myself included, to explore alternatives.

I have spent about fourteen months in Bali across four different regions, and each one suits a completely different kind of remote worker. Here is my take on where you should actually stay.

---

## Canggu: the social hub

Best for: first time Bali nomads, social butterflies, surfers

This is where most people land, and for good reason. The density of coworking spaces, cafes, gyms, and restaurants in Canggu is unmatched on the island. You can walk or scooter to everything you need. There is always something happening: events, workshops, group dinners, surf meetups.

The trade off: traffic is genuinely bad. What looks like a 10 minute drive on the map can take 40 minutes at peak hours. The main roads (Batu Bolong, Berawa, Pererenan) are gridlocked during rush hour. Rents have risen roughly 35% since 2022 as demand has surged.

Monthly budget (comfortable): $1,400 to 2,200

---

## Ubud: the wellness and focus destination

Best for: yoga, meditation, creative work, deep focus, wellness retreats

Ubud is Bali's cultural and spiritual center. It is surrounded by rice terraces, temples, and jungle. The pace is slower. The food scene is outstanding, from $2 local warungs to high end vegan restaurants. Coworking spaces like Outpost and Hubud attract a quieter, more focused crowd than Canggu's social scene.

The trade off: further from the beach (30 to 45 minutes by scooter). Quieter nightlife. More spread out. You will need a scooter. Some people find it too quiet after a few weeks. I found the quiet to be exactly the point.

Monthly budget: $1,200 to 2,000

---

## Uluwatu: the surfer's paradise

Best for: surfers, sunset chasers, people who prioritize ocean over coworking

Uluwatu sits on the Bukit Peninsula's southern cliffs, with big ocean views and some of the best surf breaks in the world. The vibe is more laid back than Canggu, with a strong surf and wellness culture. The coworking scene is smaller but growing. Spaces like Dojo Bali and Kinship Studio serve the remote work crowd.

The trade off: much more spread out than Canggu. You will need a scooter for everything. Fewer coworking options and cafes. Less social infrastructure for meeting people outside of surf culture. Not ideal if you want a dense nomad community.

Monthly budget: $1,300 to 2,000

---

## Sanur: the underrated alternative

Best for: older nomads, families, people who want calm with infrastructure

Sanur is on the east coast, away from the Canggu buzz. It is quieter, more residential, and has a calm beach with a paved path that runs for kilometers along the coast. The path is perfect for running, walking, and cycling. The coworking scene is small but functional (BWork Bali, Kevala). The expat community is older and more settled.

The trade off: much quieter nightlife. Limited coworking options. Further from the Canggu and Ubud social scenes. Not the place if you want a dense nomad social life.

Monthly budget: $1,200 to 1,800

---

## Sidemen / Amed / North Bali: the escape

Best for: deep work retreats, short getaways, people who genuinely want isolation

These areas are not practical as your primary base. You will be far from coworking spaces, reliable infrastructure, and other nomads. But for a week or two of deep work with a view of Mount Agung, Sidemen's rice terraces deliver. I go to Sidemen once a year to reset and do a week of focused writing.

## How to approach Bali

Do not commit to one area for your entire stay before arriving. Spend your first week in Canggu to meet people and get oriented. Then split your remaining time between one or two other areas based on what you discover you actually value.

Many experienced Bali nomads split their time: 2 weeks in Canggu for community, 2 weeks in Ubud for focus, 1 week somewhere quiet to reset. You do not have to pick one. I never have.

---

Canggu is the easiest place to land, but it is not necessarily the best place to stay. Bali has distinct regions for different lifestyles. Try at least two before you decide where you belong.

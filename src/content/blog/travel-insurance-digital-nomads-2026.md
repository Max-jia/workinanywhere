I have filed two insurance claims as a nomad. One was approved in 18 days. The other was denied, then overturned on appeal. The experience taught me that picking the right insurer matters a lot more than picking the cheapest one.

Travel insurance for digital nomads is not the same as vacation insurance. You are not going on a two-week trip. You are living abroad. You need coverage that works across multiple countries, handles ongoing health issues, and does not expire while you are still traveling.

I compared SafetyWing, World Nomads, and Genki, the three plans most commonly discussed in nomad communities, using 2026 pricing data and an analysis of over 500 real insurance claims.

---

## The basic difference

Each of these plans sells a different thing. Know what you are buying before you look at prices:

- **SafetyWing Nomad Insurance** is **travel insurance**. It covers emergencies, accidents, and unexpected illness. It does NOT cover routine checkups, ongoing prescriptions, or preventive care.
- **Genki Explorer** is **health insurance**. It covers both emergencies and routine care, including checkups, dental, and mental health. But it does NOT cover trip cancellation or lost luggage.
- **World Nomads Explorer** is **travel insurance** with the broadest adventure sports coverage on the market, designed for trips under 90 days, not indefinite nomad life.

These are complementary products, not direct competitors. I carry two policies myself: a travel plan for trip disruptions and emergencies, and a separate international health plan for ongoing care.

## What actually gets paid: claims data

Lazy Gals Guide analyzed over 500 real nomad insurance claims in 2026, pulling data from Reddit, forums, and firsthand reports. The claims experience differs a lot between providers:

| | SafetyWing | World Nomads | Genki |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First-submission approval rate** | 81% | 89% | **94%** |
| **Denial rate** | 19% | 11% | **6%** |
| **Appeal success rate** | 52% | 68% | 71% |
| **Typical processing time (approved)** | 18 to 21 days | 16 to 20 days | 35 to 49 days |
| **Common denial reasons** | Pre-existing conditions (28%), activity exclusions (21%) | Activity exclusions (31%), document gaps (24%) | Document language issues (35%), non-emergency classification (28%) |

Genki pays out most often but takes the longest. SafetyWing is fastest but rejects the most claims. Across all three providers, 60 to 80% of denied claims win on appeal, yet fewer than 1% of nomads bother to appeal. I learned this the hard way: always appeal a denial.

## Who should choose what

### Pick SafetyWing if:
- You are on a budget (under $70/month)
- You are a US resident (Genki is not available to US residents)
- You want simple monthly billing with no fixed end date
- You travel mostly in lower-cost countries (Southeast Asia, Latin America)
- You are under 40 and healthy
- You travel with family (two children under 10 covered free per adult)

### Pick World Nomads if:
- You do adventure sports regularly (skiing, diving, climbing, motorbike riding with a license)
- You need strong trip cancellation and interruption coverage
- Your trip is under 90 days (World Nomads is not designed for indefinite travel)
- You want a well established brand with a long track record (operating since 2002)
- You are not an EU resident (World Nomads is not available to EU residents)

### Pick Genki if:
- You are a non-US resident who wants proper long term health coverage, not just emergency travel insurance
- You want the highest coverage limits (€5M) and the best claims reliability
- You have or expect ongoing health needs (pre-existing conditions covered after 8 months; yes, you read that right)
- You visit the US and need full coverage there (no day limits on the World plan)
- You want dental and mental health included
- You are over 50 (Genki has the most age-competitive pricing)

## Things nobody tells you

1. **SafetyWing Nomad Insurance is NOT health insurance.** It covers emergencies and unexpected illness, not checkups, prescriptions, or preventive care. If you want comprehensive coverage, look at their separate Nomad Health plan ($161+/month) or Genki.

2. **Genki is NOT travel insurance.** No trip cancellation, no lost luggage. If you need those, pair Genki with a separate travel policy, or consider a different provider.

3. **Pre-existing conditions are almost never covered by travel insurance.** Genki covers them after an 8-month waiting period. SafetyWing only covers "acute onset" of pre-existing conditions, a sudden and unexpected flare-up. That is a narrow definition and you should read the fine print.

4. **Always appeal denials.** The data shows the majority of denied claims are overturned. Do not take the first no.

## FAQ

**Do I need nomad insurance or can I just use my credit card?**
Credit card travel insurance typically only covers trips under 30 to 60 days. It rarely covers pre-existing conditions, adventure sports, or routine care. If you live abroad indefinitely rather than taking a short vacation, credit card insurance is not enough. Get a dedicated nomad plan.

**Can I buy insurance after I have already left home?**
SafetyWing lets you purchase coverage after departure. World Nomads requires purchase before you leave your home country. Genki lets most nationalities buy from abroad. Check the specific requirements before you book your flight.

**What is the cheapest nomad insurance?**
SafetyWing Nomad Insurance starts at $56 a month for basic emergency coverage. Genki Explorer starts at $75 a month and includes routine care and pre-existing conditions. The pricing table above has the full breakdown.
